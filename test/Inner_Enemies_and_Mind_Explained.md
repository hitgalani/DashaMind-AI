# The 10 Inner Forces: Shadripu and Antahkarana

A simple guide to the six inner enemies and the four parts of the mind, with meaning, daily-life examples and easy ways to work on them.

---

## 1. The big picture

Your list has **10 words**. They come from two different ideas in Indian philosophy:

| Group | Words | Simple meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Shadripu** (6 inner enemies) | Kama, Krodha, Moha, Lobha, Mada, Matsarya | Six habits and feelings that pull us away from peace and good choices |
| **B. Antahkarana** (4 parts of the inner mind) | Manas, Buddhi, Chitta, Ahamkara | The four "tools" inside us that think, decide, remember and say "I" |

> **Note:** The two groups are usually taught separately. Putting them in one list of ten is a modern way to study them together. The enemies (A) are the **problems**. The mind (B) is the **machine** where those problems start and where we can fix them.

**Easy memory line:**
*"Six enemies attack. Four mind-tools decide who wins."*

## 2. Group A: Shadripu (the 6 inner enemies)

"Shad" means six. "Ripu" means enemy. These are enemies **inside** us, not outside.

### 1. Kama: Desire / Craving
- **Meaning:** Strong wanting. It is more than lust. It is any craving: "I must have this."
- **Daily life:** Cannot stop checking the phone. Wanting new things all the time. Being unhappy until you get something.
- **Why it harms:** One desire is fulfilled and a new one starts. Peace never comes.
- **Remedy:** Santosha (contentment). Ask: "Do I need this, or do I only want it?" Wait 24 hours before buying anything you crave.

### 2. Krodha: Anger
- **Meaning:** Hot, fast reaction when something blocks what we want or hurts our pride.
- **Daily life:** Shouting, rude words, slamming things, then feeling sorry later.
- **Why it harms:** Anger makes us say and do things we cannot take back. It hurts us first.
- **Remedy:** Pause before reacting. Take 10 slow breaths. Drink water. Practice Kshama (forgiveness). Talk after you calm down.

### 3. Moha: Delusion / Unhealthy attachment
- **Meaning:** Confusion and clinging. Seeing things wrongly: thinking the temporary is permanent, or holding on so tightly we cannot see clearly.
- **Daily life:** Cannot let go of old things, old hurts or one person's opinion. Making bad choices because of attachment.
- **Why it harms:** It blocks clear thinking. We cannot see the truth.
- **Remedy:** Viveka (clear discrimination). Ask: "What is real, and what is only my fear or habit?" Learn to let go slowly. Love without controlling.

### 4. Lobha: Greed
- **Meaning:** Wanting more than we need, and never feeling "enough".
- **Daily life:** Collecting more and more. Taking more than your share. Never satisfied with what you have.
- **Why it harms:** Greed makes us dishonest and anxious. A full cup still feels empty.
- **Remedy:** Daan (giving). Share small things often. Gratitude: write 3 things you already have each night.

### 5. Mada: Pride / Vanity / Arrogance
- **Meaning:** Being "drunk" on our own good points: money, looks, marks, strength, knowledge.
- **Daily life:** Showing off. Looking down on others. Not listening because "I already know."
- **Why it harms:** A proud person stops learning. People move away from them.
- **Remedy:** Vinaya (humility). Learn from everyone. Say "thank you" and "I don't know" easily. Remember: every skill came with help from others.

### 6. Matsarya: Envy / Jealousy
- **Meaning:** Pain when others succeed. Wanting others to lose what they have.
- **Daily life:** Feeling bad about a friend's good marks or good news. Comparing yourself on social media.
- **Why it harms:** It poisons friendships and steals our own joy.
- **Remedy:** Mudita (joy in others' happiness). Say "well done" and mean it. Compare yourself only with your own yesterday.

> Some books list them as Kama, Krodha, **Lobha, Moha**, Mada, Matsarya. The order changes. The six words are the same.

### Quick table

| Enemy | One-word meaning | Opposite quality (remedy) |
|---|---|---|
| Kama | Craving | Contentment (Santosha) |
| Krodha | Anger | Forgiveness (Kshama) |
| Moha | Clinging / confusion | Clear thinking (Viveka) |
| Lobha | Greed | Giving (Daan) |
| Mada | Pride | Humility (Vinaya) |
| Matsarya | Envy | Joy in others (Mudita) |

*The remedies are common practical teachings. Different teachers may name different opposites.*

## 3. Group B: Antahkarana (the 4 parts of the inner mind)

"Antah" means inner. "Karana" means instrument or tool. The inner mind is described as one tool with **four jobs**.

### 7. Manas: The thinking, wavering mind
- **Job:** Receives what the senses bring in (see, hear, touch) and produces thoughts, wishes and doubts.
- **Feels like:** "Should I? Shouldn't I?" Restless, jumping thoughts.
- **When it goes wrong:** Overthinking, distraction, doubt, a mind that follows every wish.
- **How to train it:** Daily meditation or quiet breathing (even 5 minutes). Single-tasking. Less phone scrolling.

### 8. Buddhi: Intellect / Wisdom / Decision
- **Job:** Judges, understands and **decides**. It separates right from wrong and real from unreal.
- **Feels like:** "This is the right thing to do."
- **When it goes wrong:** Using clever thinking to excuse a wrong choice or to harm others. Knowledge without good values.
- **How to train it:** Read, ask questions, reflect each night ("What did I do well? What can I improve?"), and keep good company.

### 9. Chitta: Memory / Deep consciousness
- **Job:** The storehouse. It keeps memories, habits and old impressions, including those we do not remember on purpose.
- **Feels like:** Old feelings that come up without a reason. Habits that run automatically.
- **When it goes wrong:** Old hurts and fears keep controlling today's behaviour.
- **How to train it:** Build good habits on purpose, because the storehouse learns from repetition. Heal old pain through talking, writing a journal, and forgiveness.

> **Correction on your list:** In your list, Chitta was written as "Willpower". In the standard teaching, Chitta is **memory and the store of impressions (consciousness)**, not willpower. Willpower is mostly the work of **Buddhi** (deciding) with a trained Manas (following the decision).

### 10. Ahamkara: Ego / Sense of "I"
- **Job:** Creates the feeling "I am this person, and this is mine."
- **Feels like:** "I did it." "My opinion." "I am hurt."
- **When it goes wrong:** Ego becomes pride (Mada), jealousy (Matsarya) and anger (Krodha) when "I" is touched.
- **How to train it:** Notice the words "I" and "mine". Thank others. Do work for good, not only for credit. Serve without showing off.

### Quick table

| Part | Main job | Simple word |
|---|---|---|
| Manas | Thinks, doubts, wishes | The restless mind |
| Buddhi | Understands and decides | The wise judge |
| Chitta | Stores memory and habits | The storehouse |
| Ahamkara | Says "I" and "mine" | The ego |

## 4. A famous picture: the chariot (Katha Upanishad)

Imagine a chariot:

| Part of the chariot | In us |
|---|---|
| Chariot | The body |
| Horses | The senses |
| Reins | **Manas** (mind) |
| Charioteer (driver) | **Buddhi** (intellect) |
| Rider | The Self (Atman) |

If the driver (Buddhi) is awake and the reins (Manas) are tight, the horses (senses) go the right way. If not, the horses run wild, and the six enemies take over.

## 5. How the enemies are linked (Bhagavad Gita 2.62 and 2.63)

The Gita shows a chain that explains how one small thing grows into a big problem:

```
Thinking again and again about something (Manas)
        ↓
Attachment
        ↓
Desire (Kama)
        ↓
Anger (Krodha) when desire is blocked
        ↓
Delusion (Moha)
        ↓
Memory confused (Chitta)
        ↓
Intellect lost (Buddhi)
        ↓
Fall
```

**Lesson:** Fix the problem at the **start** (what we keep thinking about). It is much easier than fixing anger at the end.

Other verses worth reading:
- **Gita 3.37:** names desire and anger as the great enemies.
- **Gita 16.21:** names desire, anger and greed as three gates to self-destruction.
- **Gita 6.5:** "Lift yourself by your own self." (Be your own friend, not your own enemy.)
- **Gita 6.35:** the restless mind is controlled by practice and detachment.

## 6. Simple daily practice (10 minutes)

**Morning (3 minutes)**
- Sit quietly. Take 10 slow breaths.
- Pick **one** quality to work on today (for example, patience).

**During the day**
- When you feel anger, desire or envy, say the name silently: *"This is Krodha."* Naming it makes it weaker.
- Pause for 3 breaths before you reply or react.

**Night (5 minutes)**
- Write 3 lines: What went well? Which enemy showed up? What will I try tomorrow?
- Write 1 thing you are grateful for.

## 8. Important notes

- Meanings differ a little between schools (Vedanta, Yoga, Samkhya) and between teachers. This file gives the common, beginner-friendly meaning.
- "Enemy" does not mean these feelings are evil. Desire, anger and pride only become a problem when they control us. A trained mind can use them wisely (for example, desire for learning, anger against injustice).
- These are ideas for self-improvement and reflection. If strong anger, sadness or worry keeps affecting your daily life, please talk with a trusted person or a counsellor.
- For deeper study, read a reliable translation of the Bhagavad Gita and the Katha Upanishad, and check what your own teacher or tradition says.
